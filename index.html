Forex Divergence Risk Simulator

Estimate what the gap between the official NBE rate and the parallel market rate could cost an import-dependent business over the next 1–12 months — as a probability, not a guess.

Sequel to the thesis: where the Markowitz model measured how a forex shock hits bank portfolio risk, this tool measures how the forex spread hits a single importer's bottom line. Methodology extends Strategic Portfolio Optimization Under a Market-Based Forex Regime (Asegahegn, 2026).

Show weekly spread fan chart

The band of plausible weekly spreads over your forecast horizon — the shaded region narrows where the model is confident, widens where regime uncertainty dominates.

How this works: weekly spreads are drawn from a two-state regime model — a "low spread" week and a "high spread" week, each with its own average and variability, switching according to fixed probabilities. On top of that, each simulated path also resamples its regime means from an estimated standard error (a simplified stand-in for a full block bootstrap), so the distribution reflects parameter uncertainty, not just week-to-week noise. This is a hand-built proxy for a proper Markov-switching model. Once 50–100 weeks of real official-vs-parallel rate data are collected, the regime means, variances, and transition probabilities will be estimated directly using statsmodels.tsa.MarkovRegression, and swapped in here without changing this interface.
